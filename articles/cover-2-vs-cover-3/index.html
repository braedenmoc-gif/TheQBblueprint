Cover 2 vs Cover 3: How Quarterbacks Tell the Difference

Every pass play starts with one question: how many safeties are deep? Answer it before the snap and you already know where the open grass is likely to be. Cover 2 and Cover 3 are two of the most common coverages you will see, and telling them apart is one of the first reads every quarterback should master.

Step one: count the deep safeties

Before you look at anything else, find the safeties. Are there two of them sitting deep, roughly over the hash marks and usually about 12 to 15 yards off the ball? Or is there one safety in the middle of the field?

Two high safeties usually means a two-deep coverage like Cover 2. One high safety usually means a one-deep coverage like Cover 3. Coaches call this "two-high" and "one-high," and it is the single most useful thing you can see before the snap. One note: two-high can also be Cover 4 (quarters). In Cover 4 the safeties usually sit a little shallower, around 10 to 12 yards, and the corners play off the ball instead of squatting.

What Cover 2 looks like

In Cover 2, the two safeties each take a deep half of the field. Underneath them, five defenders play zones: the two corners, usually squatting in the flats, and three defenders (usually linebackers) across the middle.

Pre-snap clues for Cover 2: two safeties deep and wide, and corners often lined up closer to the line, sometimes with outside leverage, ready to jam the receiver and sink to the flat.

  • Two safeties deep, each over a hash
  • Corners shallow, often outside the receiver
  • Five defenders underneath

Where Cover 2 is weak

Two defenders cannot cover the whole deep field, so Cover 2 has two soft spots. The first is the area along the sideline, deeper than the squatting corner and in front of the deep safety. Coaches call it "the hole." Corner routes and fade-stop routes attack it.

The second is the deep middle, between the two safeties. A receiver who splits them on a seam or post can get behind the linebackers and in front of the safeties.

What Cover 3 looks like

In Cover 3, one safety plays the deep middle and the two corners each take a deep outside third. That leaves four defenders underneath, usually a mix of linebackers and the other safety or nickel.

Pre-snap clues for Cover 3: one safety in the middle of the field, corners playing off the receiver with depth, and an extra defender walked down near the box.

  • One safety deep in the middle
  • Corners off the ball, ready to bail deep
  • Four defenders underneath

Where Cover 3 is weak

With three defenders deep, Cover 3 is harder to beat over the top, but the underneath zones are stretched. Quick throws to the flat, curl routes in front of the bailing corner, and seam routes that split the deep thirds all put stress on it.

Four vertical routes are a classic Cover 3 beater: four receivers going deep against three deep defenders forces the middle safety to pick one of the two seams, which leaves the other one open.

Confirm it after the snap

Defenses disguise. A two-high look can rotate into one-high at the snap, and the reverse. So treat your pre-snap read as a guess, then confirm it with your first look after the snap.

Watch the corners. If they sink and squat in the flat, think Cover 2. If they turn and run deep, think Cover 3 (or man coverage, if they stay glued to one receiver). Watch the safeties too: if one drives down toward the line late, the defense may have rotated into a one-high look.

How to practice this

Watch film with the sound off and pause every play right before the snap. Say out loud "one-high" or "two-high," then play the clip and check yourself. A few minutes a day builds the habit faster than you think.
