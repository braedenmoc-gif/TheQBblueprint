The Quarterback Pre-Snap Checklist: 5 Things to See Before Every Snap

The best quarterbacks make the game slower by doing the same thing before every snap. They look at the defense in the same order, every time, so nothing surprises them. Here is a simple five-step checklist you can start using at your next practice.

1. Count the safeties

One high or two high? This tells you the coverage family before anything else. One deep safety usually means Cover 1 or Cover 3. Two deep safeties usually means Cover 2, Cover 4 or a two-high variation.

2. Check the corners

Are the corners pressed up on your receivers or playing off, and where are their eyes? Corners pressed tight and staring at the receiver often mean man coverage. Corners playing off and looking in at you often mean zone. This changes which routes are your best options.

3. Find the Mike

Identify the middle linebacker and point him out so your offensive line knows how to set the protection. It also forces you to look at the second level of the defense, where blitzes often come from.

4. Look for blitz tells

Linebackers creeping toward the line, a safety rotating down late, or defensive backs in tight coverage with no deep help can all signal pressure.

5. Know your hot read

If the defense brings more rushers than you can block, where does the ball go? Decide this before the snap, not after. The answer is usually the area the extra rusher came from.

Make it automatic

At first, this will feel slow. That is normal. Run through the checklist on every rep at practice, and while watching film, until you do it without thinking. Once it is a habit, you will see the defense in a second or two, and the game will feel slower.
