How to Read a Blitz Before the Snap

A blitz is the defense betting that it can get to you before you get the ball out. If you see it coming, that bet usually loses. Here are the signs to look for before the snap, and what to do once you see them.

Sign 1: linebackers creeping toward the line

Linebackers usually line up a few yards off the ball, often around four to five. When one walks up into a gap, or inches forward as the play clock runs down, he may be coming. Linebackers standing right on the line over the center, in the A-gaps, are a strong warning sign.

Sign 2: a safety rotating down late

A safety who drifts toward the line of scrimmage just before the snap is often either blitzing or replacing a linebacker who is. Watch for a safety cheating down from a two-high look into the box.

Sign 3: tight coverage with no help

When defensive backs press your receivers and there is no safety deep, the defense is often playing man coverage behind a blitz. They are sending extra rushers and trusting their corners to hold up for a short time.

Sign 4: body language

Rushers often give themselves away. A linebacker leaning forward with weight on his toes, eyes on the ball instead of the backfield, or a defensive back staring at the quarterback can all signal pressure.

What to do when you see it

  • Count the rushers against your blockers. If they can bring more than you can block, someone has to throw quickly.
  • Know your hot read. Before every play, know which receiver you go to if a free rusher comes. Usually the ball goes to the area the blitzer just left.
  • Set the protection. Point out the Mike linebacker so your line knows who they are responsible for, and adjust the protection if your offense lets you.
  • Use your cadence. A hard count can make a creeping defender jump early or show his hand.
  • Get the ball out. Against a blitz, a quick completion beats holding the ball for the perfect throw.

Don't panic, and don't guess

Not every creeping linebacker blitzes. Good defenses show pressure and then back out. Use the signs to prepare, not to decide. Know your answer before the snap, then confirm what is actually coming after it.
