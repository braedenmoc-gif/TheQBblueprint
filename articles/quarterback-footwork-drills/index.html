6 Quarterback Footwork Drills You Can Do on Your Own

Accuracy starts with your feet. A good throw needs a balanced base, and a balanced base comes from footwork you have repeated so often that you do not think about it. These six drills need only a football, a few cones or markers, and some open space.

1. Line drops

Stand on a yard line as if it were the line of scrimmage. Take your three-step and five-step drops (or one-step and three-step from the shotgun), and finish each one balanced with your feet about shoulder-width apart.

Check yourself: are you landing on the balls of your feet, not your heels? Is your front shoulder pointing downfield, with your feet roughly perpendicular to the target? Do ten of each, slowly at first, then at game speed.

2. Drop and hitch

After your drop, take one or two short hitch steps forward, as if you are moving up into the pocket, then set your feet and throw. Many throws in a game come after a hitch, not straight off the drop, so practice the rhythm.

3. Climb the pocket

Set up two cones about three yards behind the line, a few yards apart, to act as edge rushers. Drop back, then step up between them, keeping your eyes downfield the whole time. Finish with a throw. This teaches you to escape edge pressure by moving forward, not by drifting backward, because the rushers' momentum carries them past you. If pressure comes up the middle instead, slide sideways rather than stepping into it.

4. Reset and throw

Set up three targets: left, middle and right. Drop back, then have a partner call out a target, or pick one at random. Reset your feet so your front shoulder points at the new target, then step toward it as you throw. Throwing off a reset base is one of the biggest keys to accuracy across the field.

5. Rollouts

Roll out to your right, square your shoulders toward the target as you run, and throw on the move. Then do the same to your left, which is harder for right-handed quarterbacks and needs more practice. Keep the ball high and close to your chest while you run.

6. Agility ladder or line hops

Quick feet help in a crowded pocket. Use an agility ladder, or just hop side to side and forward and back over a yard line, for 20 to 30 seconds at a time. Keep your knees bent and stay on the balls of your feet.

How to fit it into your week

Ten to fifteen minutes of footwork, three or four times a week, adds up quickly. Film yourself with your phone once a week so you can compare your drops over time. Small fixes to your base show up as big improvements in accuracy.
